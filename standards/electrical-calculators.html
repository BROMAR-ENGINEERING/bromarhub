Ohm's Law i
📚 What is Ohm's Law?
V = I × R — Voltage equals current times resistance. Enter any two known values and all others are calculated. Power: P = V × I.

3-Phase Power i
📚 3-Phase AC Power
Three conductors carry voltages 120° apart. P = √3 × VLL × I × PF. Australian LV: 415 V line-to-line, 230 V line-to-neutral.
VLL = line-to-line voltage  •  I = line current per phase
Formula Reference
QuantityFormula
Active Power (kW)P = √3 × VLL × I × PF ÷ 1000
Apparent Power (kVA)S = √3 × VLL × I ÷ 1000
Reactive Power (kVAr)Q = √(S² − P²)
Line Current (A)I = P × 1000 ÷ (√3 × V × PF)
Phase Voltage VLNVLN = VLL ÷ √3  (e.g. 415 ÷ 1.732 = 240 V)
Star Point — Resistive Loads i
📚 Star (Wye) Connected Resistive Loads
Each load connects between a phase and the neutral star point. Phase voltage = VLL ÷ √3. Balanced loads: neutral current = 0. Unbalanced loads: neutral carries the phasor sum of the three phase currents.
Star (Y) — ZR ZY ZB are phase impedances connected to neutral star point N. Neutral carries IN when load is unbalanced.

Transformer i
📚 Transformer Basics
Turns ratio N = Vpri ÷ Vsec. Impedance Z% limits fault current — lower Z% = higher fault current = requires higher-rated switchgear. Typical distribution transformers: Z% = 4–6%.
Formula Reference
QuantityFormula
FLC 3-PhaseI = kVA × 1000 ÷ (√3 × V)
FLC 1-PhaseI = kVA × 1000 ÷ V
Fault Current at TerminalsIfault = IFLC ÷ (Z% ÷ 100)
Turns RatioN = Vpri ÷ Vsec = Isec ÷ Ipri
Secondary Current from NIsec = Ipri × N

Prospective Fault Level i
📚 Fault Level
The prospective short-circuit current (PSC) is the maximum current in a bolted 3-phase fault. Determines switchgear breaking capacity and busbar ratings. Cable impedance between transformer and fault point reduces fault current.
